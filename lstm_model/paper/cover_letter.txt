Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript "Script-Aware Dual-Tokenization Fusion in a
Vision-Language Model for Low-Resource Indic Scene-Text Recognition" for
publication in the International Journal on Document Analysis and
Recognition, under the open topical collection "Computer Vision Systems for
Document Analysis and Recognition".

Scene-text recognition for the Brahmic scripts of South Asia trails English
by a wide margin, and one overlooked reason is tokenization: the BPE
vocabularies inherited by modern vision-language models fragment Indic
grapheme clusters into visually meaningless pieces. The manuscript makes a
vision-language recognizer (Florence-2) script-aware by injecting
grapheme-cluster tokens, and shows that the BPE-tokenized and
grapheme-tokenized decoders make largely complementary errors. A
parameter-free rule - keep the more confident decoder's output per image -
beats both branches on every dataset we test. The central evidence is a
uniform-protocol replication across twelve languages covering nine Brahmic
scripts and a Latin control: fusion exceeds the better branch in all
twelve (mean +5.0 WRR, each gain individually significant by exact McNemar
test), and which branch wins flips with the script while the fusion gain
does not. A calibration analysis explains the mechanism, and yields both a
selective-prediction operating curve for human-in-the-loop digitization
(91.4% word accuracy at 50% coverage) and a cascade that keeps 66-85% of
the fusion gain while invoking the second decoder on a minority of images.

Relative to prior work: MGP-STR fuses granularities inside a single Latin
model with learned weights; GraDeT-HTR replaces BPE with graphemes for
Bengali handwriting. Ours is, to our knowledge, the first grapheme-aware
dual-tokenization confidence fusion in a vision-language model for scene
text, evaluated across the Brahmic scripts, and it neither modifies the
architecture nor requires joint training. We compare honestly against the
strongest available specialist (the BSTD authors' PARSeq, pre-trained on
8.48M synthetic words): it dominates in-domain, while on our independently
collected test set our system is statistically indistinguishable from it
despite using three orders of magnitude less data.

We took particular care with evaluation hygiene: leakage-free by-document
splits, one shared scorer for every number, exact McNemar tests with
bootstrap confidence intervals, oracle ceilings, and reported negative
results. All datasets, splits, per-image predictions with confidences, and
the script that recomputes every number in the paper will be released upon
publication.
