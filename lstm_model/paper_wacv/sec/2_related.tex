\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Multilingual STR and Indic benchmarks.}
STR is mature for Latin script~\cite{parseq} but collapses elsewhere. IndicSTR12~\cite{indicstr12}
and the Bharat Scene Text Dataset~\cite{bstd} established real-image benchmarks for Indian
languages; on the latter, PARSeq reaches $\parseqSynth\%$ average WRR from synthetic data alone and
$\parseqFinetuned\%$ fine-tuned on real labeled images, and in-the-wild collections keep growing
the real-image supply one language at a time~\cite{banglawild}. All of it presupposes labeled
target data. Transfer \emph{between} Indian languages is established practice: Gunna et
al.~\cite{indictransfer} show that transferring among Indic scripts beats transferring from
English, exploiting the shared vowels and conjuncts our pivot formalizes, and follow-up work adds
font diversity to that recipe~\cite{indicfonts}. Both fine-tune on real images of the target
language --- the target is never absent. GlotOCR~\cite{glotocr} measures the wider problem at scale,
finding that frontier VLMs read fewer than thirty of 100+ scripts, but it scores pretrained models
on rendered text. That leaves the training-side question open: not what a model already knows, but
what it can be \emph{taught} about a script of which no real image exists. Synthetic-data engines
remain Latin-centric~\cite{unionst}, and synthetic-rendering fine-tuning has been shown for a
single low-resource script with real-data validation~\cite{manchu}. We target the regime where no
real labeled target image exists at all.

\paragraph{Tokenization granularity for complex scripts.}
MGP-STR~\cite{mgpstr} fuses character, BPE and WordPiece streams in one Latin recognizer;
grapheme tokenization beats byte-BPE for Bengali handwriting~\cite{gradet}; and fertility's value
as a tokenizer metric is contested in the LLM literature~\cite{beyondfertility}. A companion
manuscript by the same authors~\cite{parallelsub}, anonymized in the supplement, makes the
\emph{supervised} grapheme-vs-BPE choice predictable across nine scripts (polarity \lawPolarity,
leave-one-script-out $\lawLoso\%$); the present paper shares no experiment with it, and tests, then
refutes, the conjecture that the same property predicts zero-real-image transfer.

\paragraph{Unseen units vs.\ unseen scripts.}
A substantial line reads unseen \emph{characters within a known script} by decomposition into
shared sub-units: stroke- and radical-based zero-shot Chinese
recognition~\cite{strokezs,star,hiercode} and open-set recognition of novel
characters~\cite{openset1,openset2,moose}. Zero-shot \emph{detection} of unseen scripts localizes
but does not read~\cite{unseendet}. Byte-level pivots let ASR cover unseen-script
languages~\cite{maestrou}, and task-arithmetic analogies achieve zero-real-data synthetic-to-real
HTR for five \emph{Latin-script} languages, leaving unseen writing systems explicitly
open~\cite{taskanalogies}. To our knowledge no prior system reads real scene text of an entirely
unseen script --- different Unicode block, different glyphs --- from zero real target images.

\paragraph{What predicts cross-lingual transfer.}
In NLP, transfer tracks lexical and subword overlap rather than language
relatedness~\cite{subwordoverlap,overlap2}. For STR specifically, Ref.~\cite{crosslingstr}
concludes that source dataset size and visual similarity, not linguistic typology, drive
cross-lingual transfer. Our findings sharpen rather than contradict this, in a regime that work
did not study (zero real target images): (i) a typology-adjacent structural property, fertility,
indeed fails as a transfer predictor ($\rho=\spearFert$), as a data-centric view expects;
(ii) \emph{synthetic target exposure} dominates, which is a target-side data-size effect;
(iii) within a fixed budget, coverage of the shared grapheme space orders transfer
($\rho=\spearCovEqual$ among equal-budget scripts), an OCR instantiation of the subword-overlap
principle in a \emph{designed} output space; and (iv) a frozen-encoder visual-similarity control
predicts nothing in our data ($\rho=\spearVissim$). Because LOSO balances source data by construction,
the confound identified by~\cite{crosslingstr} is controlled by design.

\paragraph{Prediction and preregistration in empirical ML.}
Scaling laws make supervised STR predictable in model and data size~\cite{strscaling};
preregistration and negative-result reporting are the reproducibility literature's recommended
remedies for HARKing and cherry-picking~\cite{preregml,repro}, yet full-study adoption is rare.
We run the entire campaign under such a preregistration and score every forecast it filed.
