\begin{abstract}
Scene-text recognition (STR) is effectively solved for Latin script yet fails for most of the
world's writing systems, and no benchmark measures the case that matters for the long tail:
reading a script for which \emph{no real labeled image exists at all}. We contribute an
\emph{evaluation protocol} for exactly this setting, and we run a forecasting campaign inside it
that is adversarial to our own hypotheses. The protocol is leave-one-script-out over nine Brahmic
scripts of a public real-image benchmark: the held-out script is seen only as rendered synthetic
images, never a real photograph, and is read in a shared grapheme \emph{pivot} space; an
out-of-benchmark rung extends it to Khmer, a disjoint Unicode block outside the benchmark. Every
quantitative forecast was filed \emph{prospectively} --- committed and pushed before the run it
predicts --- and every one is scored here, including the misses.

Under the protocol, a compact open vision--language model reads every held-out script
(\wrrBmin--\wrrBmax\% word recognition, character accuracy up to \chaBbengali\%) from source-only
baselines of \wrrAtamil--\wrrAdevanagari\%. We anchor that to interpretable references measured on
the identical crops under the identical metric: stock per-script Tesseract, trained on real labeled
target-script images, is beaten on \tessScriptsWon\ of nine (\wrrBmean\% vs.\ \tessMean\%
macro-average), while a prompted 7B frontier VLM, scored with every generosity, reads only Bengali
and Devanagari (\vlmWrrBengali/\vlmWrrDevanagari\%) and loses on \vlmScriptsWon\ of nine.
The benchmark authors' own supervised specialists, trained on real labeled target images, reach
\parseqMean\% on the same crops, of which we recover \oursOverCeiling.
A controlled ablation isolates the pivot --- not fine-tuning per se --- as the transfer mechanism:
replacing it with the stock BPE tokenizer under identical data, seed and recipe degrades all nine
scripts ($\bpeRatioMin\times$--$\bpeRatioMax\times$; sign test $p=\bpeSignP$), on three despite
equal-or-better character accuracy.

The forecasting record is where this campaign is most informative, and it is largely negative. The
preregistered primary hypothesis (tokenizer fertility predicts transfer) is \emph{falsified}
(Spearman $\spearFert$). A prospectively filed dose--response is \emph{log-linear with a
script-invariant slope} ($\scaleSlope$ WRR/doubling) but the pre-committed magnitude missed by
$\scaleSlopeRatio\times$. Pivot-space coverage orders scripts in-sample and fails out-of-sample
($r=\covOOSr$). Khmer, forecast at \khmerPred\% before any Khmer recognizer existed, returned
\khmerB\% --- a $\khmerResidRMSE\sigma$ miss that also locates the method's boundary. A
from-scratch CRNN replicates the direction but not the magnitude. We therefore advance no
predictive law. The protocol, the fully scored prospective-forecast record, and a machine-checked
verification chain --- released in full --- are the contribution; the pivot is the evidence.
\end{abstract}
