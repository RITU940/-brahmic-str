\begin{abstract}
Scene-text recognition is solved for Latin script and fails for most of the world's writing
systems, yet no benchmark scores the case that decides the long tail: a script for which \emph{no
real labeled image exists at all}. We contribute a protocol for that case and the infrastructure to
audit it --- not a new dataset. It is leave-one-script-out over nine Brahmic scripts of a public
benchmark: the held-out script appears only as rendered synthetic images, never a photograph, and
is read in a round-trip-lossless shared grapheme \emph{pivot} space, so pivot accuracy is
target-script accuracy. An out-of-benchmark rung extends it to Khmer, a disjoint Unicode block. We
release the pivot construction, the rendering pipeline, every split and result, and a harness that
re-derives all \verifyMacros\ numbers here and fails on mismatch; a tenth script needs only a
correspondence map and a font.

Applied to a compact open vision--language model, the protocol makes every held-out script readable
--- \wrrBmin--\wrrBmax\% word recognition from source-only baselines of
\wrrAtamil--\wrrAdevanagari\% --- and scales it against three reference systems on the identical
crops under the identical metric: stock per-script Tesseract, trained on real target-script
images, loses on \tessScriptsWon\ of nine (\wrrBmean\% vs.\ \tessMean\%); a prompted
7B VLM loses on \vlmScriptsWon\ of nine; the benchmark authors' supervised specialists reach
\parseqMean\%, of which we recover \oursOverCeiling. Swapping the pivot for a stock BPE tokenizer
under identical data, seed and recipe degrades all nine scripts (sign test $p=\bpeSignP$),
isolating output-space structure as the mechanism.

Every forecast was committed before the run it predicts, and scoring them is what makes such an
apparatus informative rather than decorative --- it rules things out. The preregistered primary,
that tokenizer fertility predicts transfer, is \emph{falsified} ($\rho=\spearFert$); a filed
dose--response is log-linear with a script-invariant slope but missed its committed magnitude by
$\scaleSlopeRatio\times$; Khmer, forecast before any Khmer recognizer existed, returned \khmerB\%,
a $\khmerResidRMSE\sigma$ miss that locates the method's boundary. We advance no predictive law --- we advance the
protocol that refuted these claims, the infrastructure to check it, and a scored record of what it
ruled out.
\end{abstract}
